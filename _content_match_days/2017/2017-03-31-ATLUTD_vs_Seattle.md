Atlanta United leave Seattle with a point and a clean sheet from a match that never gave either attack much room to breathe. The 0-0 score fits the night. Atlanta had enough of the ball to play through stretches of pressure, finishing with 384 completed passes from 485 attempts, but the final touch around goal never matched the work that came before it. Four of Atlanta’s 11 shots were blocked, four went wide, and none became the breakthrough the match needed.

---
## 1st Half

{% include centered_image.html
   file="2017-03-31 - C8S8GeHUIAU9nsa.webp"
   caption="( Photo by ATLUTD )"
%}

Atlanta’s first half was mostly about getting through Seattle’s pressure without letting the game tilt too far. The team found passing rhythm in pieces, but the attack struggled to turn possession into clean looks. Seattle kept the middle crowded, and Atlanta’s best moments needed one more sharp action near the box.

The cards changed the feel of the half. Julian Gressel was booked in the 19th minute, and Yamil Asad followed in the 42nd, which put two important wide players on a warning before halftime. Atlanta still kept the game steady enough to reach the break at 0-0, but those yellows took some bite out of the pressure and forced the team to defend with more care.

---
## 2nd Half

{% include centered_image.html
   file="2017-03-31 - C8XA-IgUQAAIAjZ.webp"
   caption="( Photo by ATLUTD )"
%}

Seattle made the first attacking move in the 56th minute, bringing on Clint Dempsey for Álvaro Fernández. That was the moment when the match could have started to lean toward the home side, but Atlanta answered without changing the whole shape. Miguel Almirón came on for Jacob Peterson in the 64th minute and gave Atlanta a better carrier through midfield, which was important in a game where the next pass was often harder than the first one.

Tata Martino’s next two changes were about closing the match with the right kind of balance. Carlos Carmona replaced Chris McCann in the 72nd minute, giving Atlanta fresh legs and more edge in midfield. Kenwyne Jones replaced Tito Villalba in the 81st, which gave Atlanta a more direct outlet as the game became stretched late.

Seattle pushed its own bench late, bringing on Henry Wingo in the 79th minute and Will Bruin in the 90th. Atlanta handled that final push without losing the clean sheet. The final whistle came in the 93rd minute, and Atlanta had earned the point through a full night of disciplined defending and steady game management.

---
## Closing Thoughts

Atlanta leave Seattle with a point because the defensive work held up when the attack never fully clicked. The 11 shots show that Atlanta were able to get into decent areas, but the chances rarely felt clean. Too many final actions were crowded, rushed, or met by Seattle bodies in front of goal.

The clean sheet is the part that travels. Atlanta had to manage two first-half yellow cards, handle Seattle’s second-half changes, and keep the match from turning into the kind of late scramble that usually favors the home team. They did that without losing their shape or giving the game away.

Leandro González Pírez gave Atlanta a steady night at the back, completing 50 of 60 passes while helping keep the defensive line connected. Jeff Larentowicz gave the midfield a reliable base, and Miguel Almirón’s second-half minutes gave Atlanta a little more composure on the ball when the game needed it.

This was not Atlanta at its sharpest going forward. It was Atlanta being mature enough to take something from a difficult road match. In Seattle, that is enough.
