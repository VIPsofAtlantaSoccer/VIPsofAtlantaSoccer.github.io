Atlanta United left BMO Field with a 2-2 draw after a difficult night against Toronto FC. Tito Villalba scored both Atlanta goals, first giving the Five Stripes the lead and then pulling them level just after halftime. Toronto had more of the ball and more of the chances, while Atlanta had to protect the point late after a Yamil Asad red card.

Toronto finished with 20 shots to Atlanta’s six and 440 total passes to Atlanta’s 413. Atlanta did not create much, but it made its chances count and held on when the final stretch turned against them.

---
## {{ site.data.language.match_day_1st_half }}

Atlanta kicked off through Miguel Almirón, but the first meaningful moment went against the Five Stripes. Jeff Larentowicz was booked in the 9th minute, giving Atlanta an early disciplinary issue in midfield.

Atlanta still found the first goal. Villalba opened the scoring in the 14th minute, finishing from outside the box to give the Five Stripes a 1-0 lead.

But the lead disappeared quickly. Villalba was booked in the 17th minute, and two minutes later Sebastian Giovinco pulled Toronto level at 1-1. Atlanta had struck first, but Toronto’s response kept the match from settling into Atlanta’s preferred rhythm.

Toronto eventually turned its pressure into the halftime lead. Justin Morrow scored in the 43rd minute, completing the first-half comeback and sending Atlanta into the break down 2-1.

Toronto restarted through Giovinco, but Atlanta answered right away. Villalba scored in the 46th minute to make it 2-2, turning the match from a chase into something Atlanta could manage.

For a while, that was enough. Toronto still had more of the ball and more of the shot volume, but Atlanta had steadied the score and kept the game within reach.

That changed in the 74th minute when Yamil Asad was sent off. From there, Atlanta’s job became clear. Protect the point, stay compact, and find outlets when Toronto’s pressure pinned them back.

The substitutions followed that shift. Chris McCann added a safer midfield option, Kenwyne Jones replaced Villalba as a target higher up the field, and Harrison Heath came on in stoppage time as Atlanta worked through the final minutes.

Toronto kept searching for a winner, but Atlanta held its shape and saw out the 2-2 draw.

---
## {{ site.data.language.match_day_ending }}

Atlanta did not control the match, but it controlled enough of the decisive moments to leave with a point. Villalba gave the Five Stripes the finishing Toronto lacked, and the group found enough structure after Asad’s red card to keep the draw from slipping away.

That made the result more valuable than the performance. Atlanta spent long stretches under pressure, created little, and still left BMO Field with something. On a night shaped by Toronto’s volume and Atlanta’s late disadvantage, 2-2 was a hard-earned road point.
