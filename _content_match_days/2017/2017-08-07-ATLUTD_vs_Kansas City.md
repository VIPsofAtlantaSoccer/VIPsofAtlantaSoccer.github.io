Atlanta arrived in Kansas City short-handed. Josef Martinez, Kenwyne Jones, Alec Kann, Mark Bloom, and Greg Garza were unavailable, leaving Tata Martino without two strikers, his starting left back, and one of his goalkeepers.

Sporting KC entered with the league’s best defensive record, while Atlanta arrived with MLS’s highest-scoring attack by goals per game. Kansas City had allowed 15 goals through 22 matches. Atlanta had scored 41 through 21.

Sporting KC was unbeaten at Children’s Mercy Park.  Atlanta was better at home than on the road.

## {{ site.data.language.match_day_1st_half }}

Sporting KC kept the opening stretch compact. Kansas City closed the middle, kept numbers behind the ball, and forced Atlanta into wide play.  Almirón dropped into pockets to find the ball, Villalba looked for space behind the back line, and Asad worked from the left, but Atlanta had little room around the box.

Larentowicz went into the book in the 20th minute.  Carmona followed three minutes later.  Atlanta now had both holding midfielders on yellow cards, with Kansas City still trying to pull the game through the center of the field.

Kansas City had the only shot on target of the first half. Guzan handled it in the 33rd minute, keeping the match scoreless. Atlanta’s best openings came when Sporting KC lost the ball in central areas, but Kansas City recovered quickly enough to stop those breaks from becoming clear chances.

Opara was booked in the 26th minute, giving Kansas City its own early yellow. The rest of the half stayed narrow. Sporting KC kept Atlanta from finding much room around the box, and Atlanta reached halftime level despite the early cards in midfield. 

Kansas City also had a first-half penalty shout for an Atlanta handball in the box. Vermes was asked afterward if he expected VAR to step in.

## {{ site.data.language.match_day_2nd_half }}

Sporting KC brought on Feilhaber for Cristian Lobato at halftime, giving them another technical player in midfield and a set-piece option around the box.

   file="2017-08-06 - DGp805KUIAEe0od.webp"

Asad went into the book in the 50th minute, Atlanta’s third yellow card of the night.  In the 58th minute, Blessing drew a penalty for Sporting KC.  Feilhaber stepped to the spot and converted, putting Kansas City ahead 1-0.

González Pírez went into the book in the 60th minute, adding another card to a night that was already getting tense for Atlanta. Six minutes later, Martino turned to Peterson. The substitution brought Peterson back onto the field for the first time since March, and it happened in Kansas City, against the club where he had spent five seasons.

Martino brought on McCann and Vázquez as Atlanta pushed for an equalizer.

Atlanta’s equalizer came from the kind of play Peterson had been describing. Vázquez fought for the ball in the box and kept the chance alive. Peterson read the loose ball, ran onto it six yards from goal, and finished against his former club.

Atlanta snatched a point late. Kansas City can only view it as two dropped points.

Atlanta entered with the league’s highest-scoring attack by goals per game, but finished with seven shots and two on target. Sporting KC did not need to open the game to control it. It kept Atlanta in narrow areas, limited the space around Almirón, Villalba, and Asad, and made the night look more like a Sporting KC match than an Atlanta United match.

ATLUTD’s starting attack had a quiet night by its usual standard. The equalizer came from the bench, with Vázquez keeping the play alive and Peterson finishing it. That gave Atlanta a point from a night when its first-choice attacking group never really took over.

Atlanta left one of the league’s toughest road stadiums with something.  It showed that Atlanta had enough depth and late-game persistence to steal a point when needed.
