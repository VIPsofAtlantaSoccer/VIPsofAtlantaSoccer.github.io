Atlanta United did enough early to put itself in position for a road result, but the match changed in one brutal stretch before halftime. Kenwyne Jones gave Atlanta the lead, Ignacio Piatti pulled Montréal level from the spot, and Leandro González Pírez’s red card turned the second half into a survival exercise.  And Atlanta couldn't hold out.  Anthony Jackson-Hamel scored in 2nd half stoppage, putting Montréal ahead 2-1.

Montréal finished with 55% possession, 22 shots, six corners, and the bulk of the attacking momentum, while Atlanta managed six shots and forced no saves from Evan Bush. The red card changed the second half for both teams.  Atlanta would defence, Montréal would attack.

---
## 1st Half

The match’s first highlight came from Montréal’s back line. Víctor Cabrera was booked for a foul in the opening 15', then left injured shortly afterward.  Montréal was forced into an early sub with Chris Duvall entering into the match. For Atlanta used the opportunity to repeatedly test their defense.

Atlanta eventually took advantage by way of Tito Villalba speed and directness.  He supplied a ball to Kenwyne Jones, who finished in the 39th minute, putting Atlanta ahead 1-0 prior of the break.  

But the comfort of a halftime lead never arrived.  Matteo Mancosu won a penalty before halftime, which Piatti converted to make it 1-1.  Atlanta were close to taking a lead into the locker room, but instead Montréal had restored the match and regained belief before the break.

The half then turned decisively against Atlanta. González Pírez was sent off in first-half stoppage time for a professional foul, leaving Atlanta down a man for a full half. 

Both halftime speeches were now obsolete.  Atlanta were now forced to try and protect the draw.  While Montréal could attack with gusto.

## 2nd Half

Tata Martino made the first necessary adjustment at halftime.  Anton Walkes replaced Julian Gressel, giving Atlanta another defender and making the plan clear.  The second half was going to be about protecting space, limiting central chances, and trying to leave Stade Saputo with a point.

Montréal took control of the ball immediately and kept Atlanta pinned back for long stretches.  The home side finished the half with 68% possession, outshot Atlanta 13-1, and completed 252 passes to Atlanta’s 121.  Atlanta were restricted to clearing pressure, resetting the block, and looking for rare moments to breathe.

Mauro Biello made the first attacking move from the bench in the 62nd minute, bringing on Ballou Tabla for Calum Mallace.  Montréal already had the ball and the extra man, and the change gave them another direct player to run at an Atlanta side that was spending more and more time defending its box.

Martino’s response in the 73rd minute followed Atlanta’s game state.  Kevin Kratz replaced Kenwyne Jones, taking away the central striker but adding another midfielder to help Atlanta protect the center of the pitch.  Atlanta became even more defensive in the 80th minute when Chris McCann replaced Tito Villalba.  By then, the earlier threat from the attackers had mostly disappeared.  Atlanta no longer had the numbers to support those breaks consistently, and every forward run risked leaving the team stretched behind the ball.

Montréal made the decisive attacking change three minutes later.  Anthony Jackson-Hamel replaced Dominic Oduro in the 83rd minute, giving Montréal a fresh forward against an Atlanta back line that had spent the half absorbing pressure.  Atlanta nearly survived it, but the hosts were still pushing towards one final chance.

Atlanta kept trying to take air out of the match.  Alec Kann received a late yellow card for time wasting, but it was not enough.  Jackson-Hamel had only been on the field for a few minutes, but he scored in the 92nd minute from Hernán Bernardello’s assist to give Montréal a 2-1 lead.  Montréal had spent the half forcing Atlanta deeper and deeper, and Atlanta eventually ran out of room.

---
## Closing Thoughts

This was not a simple blown lead.  Atlanta had a workable plan for an away game.  It took the lead through Kenwyne Jones and forced Montréal to chase.  Jones gave Atlanta the center-forward moment it needed, and Tito Villalba’s assist showed how dangerous Atlanta could be when it had space to run.

The match turned completely just before halftime.  Piatti’s penalty erased the lead, and González Pírez’s red card changed every decision that followed.  Tata Martino was forced into defensive moves.  Atlanta bunkered and were close to getting out of town with a point.  But Montréal kept pushing bodies forward until Atlanta finally broke.

Bernardello tied the ending together.  He was named player of the match and delivered the assist for Jackson-Hamel’s stoppage-time winner, giving Montréal the final pass after a second half built on sustained pressure.  The 2-1 loss should be remembered as a match Atlanta almost rescued after playing too long without control, without an outlet, and with one fewer player.
