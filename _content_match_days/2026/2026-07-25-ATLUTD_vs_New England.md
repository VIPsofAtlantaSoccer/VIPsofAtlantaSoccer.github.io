Three nights after falling two goals behind Charlotte, Atlanta United sent almost the same lineup onto the field at **Gillette Stadium**.  Atlanta again trailed 2-0 at halftime.  This time, the Five Stripes didn't come back.

Stian Gregersen replaced Enea Mihaj. Fafà Picault remained at center forward.  Tristan Muyumba, Will Reilly and Cooper Sanchez made up the midfield.  Jorge Theiler stood on the touchline while Tata Martino served a yellow-card suspension.

New England scored twice in each half to win 4-1 Saturday night in Foxborough.  Atlanta finished with 17 shots to New England’s 11 and put seven on target, but relied heavily on Miguel Almirón and Alexey Miranchuk to produce bits of magic.  ATLUTD produced little resembling a cohesive, organized attack.

{% include centered_image.html

Muyumba stood on the ball near Atlanta’s back line in the fifth minute, struggling to find a target.  He eventually carried the ball forward and rolled a light pass towards Picault, forcing him to react with a defender already on him.  Picault lost it immediately.  That sequence was typical of Atlanta’s night.  The pass came late, Picault was already under pressure, and the players never looked on the same page.

Miranchuk and Almirón tried to combine near the box, but three New England players quickly surrounded them and ended the attack.

Almirón drove forward soon after with no teammate making a run into the box.  He rushed a shot while falling, struck an Atlanta player and recovered the loose ball before forcing Matt Turner into a save.  

New England went ahead less than a minute later.  Turner started the move with a quick throw, Dor Turgeman switched the field and Matt Polster found Carles Gil outside the box.  Muyumba closed him down, but Gil sent him the wrong way and down tot he pitch.  Gil tapped it centrally to the arc, opened onto his left foot, and curled a perfect shot beyond Lucas Hoyos into the far side of the net.

Atlanta’s defensive shape was becoming difficult to identify. Alonso, Reilly and Gregersen formed a misaligned back three, while Báez remained higher on the left. It was unclear whether Báez was meant to play as a fullback or midfielder, or who was responsible for organizing the line.

Almirón continued doing most of the work to move Atlanta forward. He beat players on the left and carried the ball inside, only for the possession to end with a teammate losing it.  Báez later passed to Reilly under immediate pressure, while Muyumba found Picault resulting in a dangerous ball across the six-yard box that nobody attacked.  Muyumba tried to release Almirón soon after, but the run never came and the pass rolled into empty space. 

Muyumba stepped in to break up another New England move and Yusuf slid in hard, spinning him around and sending him heavily to the ground. Yusuf was shown a yellow card. Muyumba had stopped the ball and made sure he won the foul.

The confusion was just as visible without the ball. Brooklyn Raines went down during a New England attack and four Atlanta players stopped around him in a stare down of 'who wants the ball'.  Instead, Raines got back to his feet with the ball and continued forward while they watched.  Alonso eventually headed the cross clear.

Báez made one of Atlanta’s better defensive plays on New England’s next attack.  He stayed with Peyton Miller down the right and reached across as Miller entered the box.  Báez taps it just enough to prevent Miller from being able to shoot.

By then, Atlanta were looking for hero ball. The Five Stripes wanted the breakaway, the through ball or the individual moment of brilliance rather than moving together as a collective.  

At one point, Picault became frustrated enough to shout toward the midfield and defense to move the ball faster. It was another visible sign of disharmony within the team.

Alonso carried the ball down the left and found Almirón, who sent it into the box toward Picault. Picault nearly reached it before New England cleared.  Edwards was booked moments later after arriving late on Turgeman, and Hoyos had to rush off his line to collect a through ball before Yusuf could get there.

Gregersen then found Miranchuk near midfield with a long pass.  Miranchuk carried toward the arc but had no shooting lane, so he moved the ball right to Reilly. Reilly ran onto it and shot, but New England blocked the attempt.  The corner was headed back toward Muyumba, who had time to bring the ball down but struck it first-time and sent it wide.

New England took the corner short, bringing two players towards the flag to make sure they kept possession.  The Revolution passed their way into the right side of Atlanta’s box, where Yusuf drove a beautiful low ball across goal.  It could almost have been a shot, but Turgeman was already running towards the near post.  Reilly followed the run but remained behind Turgeman the entire way.   Hoyos was focused only on the incoming ball and not looking for runners.  Turgeman arrived first, redirected it off the right post and into the net.

New England began passing through Atlanta with little resistance after the second goal. Gregersen eventually fouled Turgeman near midfield to stop one move, while Muyumba broke up another attack on the right.

Atlanta continued looking towards Almirón. He found space on the left and sent another dangerous cross along the six-yard box, but nobody arrived to meet it or even pressure Turner. Muyumba then slipped a through ball behind the defense. Almirón reached it under pressure and managed a soft left-footed touch towards goal, only for the ball to bounce off the post and back towards Turner.

Miranchuk found him centrally soon after. Almirón drove a hard shot from outside the arc towards the upper corner, forcing Turner to reach up and tip it over.  A beautiful shot.

Atlanta produced two more dangerous deliveries before halftime. Edwards sent a high ball into an excellent central position, but Picault was the only Atlanta player in the box and was easily knocked away from it.  Almirón and Báez later combined on the left before finding Picault, who drove another ball across the six-yard box.  Once again, nobody made the run.

The half ends 2-0 New England.

Atlanta returned with the same players and immediately looked vulnerable.  Báez played the ball back to Hoyos under pressure.  Hoyos tried to move the ball left before clearing it, but dribbled over the end line and gave New England a corner.  

Brooklyn Raines carried the ball through a group of Atlanta players, moving fluidly while the defenders remained static around him.  He was free to get a cross into the box.

Atlanta briefly found some movement at the other end. Miranchuk and Almirón combined in New England’s half, although Almirón’s attempted chip towards Báez carried too far.  Muyumba then used a backheel to release Almirón, who moved it to Miranchuk before Picault tried to return the ball into the box. New England headed it clear.

The next combination broke down with a poor pass from Miranchuk, allowing New England to counter into the space behind him.  Will Sands turned Edwards inside the box and chipped a ball across the six-yard area.  The header went over, but New England should have scored.

Reilly was booked moments later for fouling Turgeman.  New England then sent Zambrano over the top, forcing Edwards into a physical race towards goal before the offside flag stopped the attack. Edwards recovered well on the next move, spinning away from pressure and clearing the ball after Yusuf and Zambrano had attacked down the left.

Miranchuk and Almirón failed to connect again when Miranchuk tried to release Miggy, but the pass went nowhere near him.

New England made it 3-0 in the 56'.  Sanchez tried to play a through ball, but the Revolution intercepted it and immediately broke towards the left.  Gil found Yusuf, and New England used a series of quick, pinball passes to move through Atlanta’s defense.  Yusuf drove the ball across the box, and Peyton Miller arrived unmarked at the back post for a simple tap-in.  Atlanta was thoroughly outclassed on this play.

Yusuf rolled his ankle during the sequence and was replaced by Jackson Yueill. Atlanta also made a change, removing Muyumba and sending on Luke Brennan.

ATLUTD changed its shape with the sub.  Brennan started on the right, with Miranchuk moving centrally.

Atlanta started dropping deeper and trying to break forward, but the plan was not working.  When they did spring forward, Edwards sent a long ball up the right towards Brennan, who reached it just before it went out and crossed into the box.  Nobody made the run.

Báez pulled Miller down to stop another break.  New England then sent Yueill over the top, forcing Hoyos to come out and collect.  Atlanta was still unable to anticipate whether an attack would come through midfield, over the back line or from the wing.

Brennan carried the ball forward and searched for a pass.  With no opening ahead of him, he turned back, was clipped from behind and went down.  No foul was given.

New England took possession and moved quickly from left to right before finding Raines running centrally.  Raines slipped the ball to an open Jackson Yueill near the edge of the box and continued his run.  Yueill returned it into his path, and Raines stepped onto the pass before slotting a left-footed shot into the lower corner.

Atlanta had been thoroughly outmatched. New England moved the ball across the pitch, completed the give-and-go and finished the sequence without Atlanta disrupting any part of it.

Atlanta replaced Edwards with Ronald Hernández, but continued directing most of its play through Almirón and Miranchuk.  Hernández found space on the right without seeing much of the ball, while Harris was booked after pulling Miranchuk backwards to stop another move through the middle.

Miranchuk continued trying to move Atlanta forward.  He stole the ball and found Picault, who advanced before sending a weak pass into the middle for New England to clear.  Picault is clearly gassed at this point.  

Miranchuk on the left, sees Brennan in the box. he sends a cross in.  But Turner gets there before Brennan.

New England kept sending players forward in coordinated attacks.  Griffin Yow drove a powerful shot towards goal, forcing Hoyos into a strong save and a corner.  

Calvin Harris appeared to catch Gregersen in the face with a high boot.  No foul was called, and the play continued without a VAR intervention.  Gregersen went down off the ball soon after. He got back to his feet, but could barely walk.  Atlanta quickly prepared a sub.

Mihaj replaced Gregersen, and Cayman Togashi replaced Picault. Picault had been out of gas for most of the second half.

Hernández continued finding open space on the right, but Atlanta rarely passed to him. The team remained intent on working through Almirón and Miranchuk in the middle.

Brennan kept providing energy. He stripped Yow near the sideline to win a throw, then won the ball again and sent it forward to Miranchuk before continuing his run. Báez followed with a hard shot that Turner saved.

Almirón passed to Miranchuk, who found Togashi centrally. Togashi returned it first-touch, Miranchuk moved it back into Almirón’s path inside the box.  And Almirón drove a hard shot past Turner.  New England could not move quickly enough to keep up with Atlanta’s pinball passing.  It was Atlanta’s best team move of the night and came only after Togashi entered to give Almirón and Miranchuk a central player to combine with.

A final ball over the top exposed another communication problem between Mihaj and Alonso.  Both arrived to stop it, but neither immediately decided who should take possession.  Alonso eventually cleared.

Atlanta continued trying to combine around New England’s box until the final whistle.  Almirón stretched for one final pass, went down and remained on the turf as the move ended.

## {{ site.data.language.match_day_ending }}

New England played as a single collective.  Gil, Turgeman and Raines each finished with a goal and an assist, Yusuf supplied two assists and Yueill added another from the bench.  All four Revolution goals included at least one credited assist, with seven primary and secondary assists distributed across six players.  New England’s players ran beyond the ball, returned passes and gave the player in possession somewhere to go.  The Revs scored from a quick transition, a short corner, a low ball across the box and a give-and-go through the middle.  Different players supplied the pass, made the run and finished the move.

Atlanta looked like a collection of individuals trying to create a bit of magic.  Miranchuk repeatedly dropped towards the back line to receive the ball.  Almirón carried it through pressure and tried to reach the box.  Too many possessions depended on one of the two Designated Players beating a defender, finding the next pass and then continuing forward to finish the move.  They cannot be asked to do it all.

Atlanta’s only goal showed what could happen when another player joined them up top. Almirón found Miranchuk, Togashi returned the ball first-time and Miranchuk put Almirón through inside the box.  It was Togashi’s first assist of the season and Atlanta’s cleanest collective move of the night.

That sequence also exposed what Atlanta is missing.  Picault worked until he was exhausted, but he did not provide the consistent presence through the middle needed to connect the attack.  Almirón and Miranchuk need a forward who can occupy defenders, receive the ball under pressure, make the return pass and finish chances of his own.  Atlanta remains sorely short of a Designated Player-level presence at striker.

The overall numbers made the match appear closer than it was. Atlanta held 51.6 percent possession, attempted 17 shots and completed 484 passes. New England scored 4 goals from 11 shots, and finished with 1.27 xG to Atlanta’s 0.68.  

Almirón described the performance as an ongoing failure.
