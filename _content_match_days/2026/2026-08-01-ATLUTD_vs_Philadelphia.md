Atlanta played more than an hour with 10 men, scored twice and carried a 2-0 lead until Philadelphia pulled one back in the 73rd minute.  Lucas Hoyos stopped almost everything sent towards him.  Miguel Almirón fought through two defenders for the opening goal.  Tristan Muyumba curled in a second that briefly made the impossible look real.

And then, six minutes into eight minutes of stoppage time, Philadelphia scored twice in barely a minute to win 3-2.

Neil Pierre headed in the equalizer in the 96th minute. Before Atlanta could catch its breath, Ezekiel Alladoh stretched out his left foot and redirected Kai Wagner's cross past Hoyos for the winner in the 97th.  Philadelphia had turned a two-goal deficit into a 3-2 victory, while Atlanta's players were left standing inside the box trying to understand how everything had disappeared so quickly.

Atlanta entered the match already struggling and changed to a back five against a Philadelphia team willing to attack through both wings.  Enea Mihaj's disputed red card in the 26th minute forced everyone else into a desperate defensive exercise.  Atlanta nearly survived it.  Instead, another loss left the Five Stripes with a 3-12-3 record and 12 points, firmly leading the race for the MLS wooden spoon.

## {{ site.data.language.match_day_1st_half }}

The first 15 minutes set the tone for the night.  Philadelphia had Atlanta on the back foot, attacking through both fullbacks and forcing Picault to keep dropping deep to help.  Atlanta found a few promising combinations, but could not hold onto the ball long enough to turn them into sustained attacks.

Reilly's giveaway sent Iloski through in the 17th minute, but Hoyos read the danger early and rushed off his line to keep Atlanta level.  

In the 26th minute, Iloski chased the ball towards the sideline with Mihaj behind him.  Mihaj reacted by reaching in and clipping his heel.  Allen Chapman was about 10 yards away and went straight to his back pocket.  The replays were not clear. If none of the available angles showed enough to overturn Chapman's decision, then the red card had to stand. At halftime, the broadcast reported that the contact was high, along the Achilles.  In either case, Mihaj gave Chapman a decision to make. There was no reason to reach in from behind with Iloski heading away from goal. It was a poor decision that cost Atlanta.  Atlanta had 63 minutes to survive with 10 men.

Four minutes later, Miranchuk headed the ball towards midfield and Picault flicked it over Philadelphia's back line.  Almirón took off after it with Westfield and Pierre closing from either side.  The two defenders squeezed him as Blake rushed out, and Almirón went down in the collision.  Almirón stumbled through the contact and started to go down, with the first instinct that Chapman might have a decision to make. Instead, the ball kept bouncing forward. Almirón managed to toe-poke it as he fell, and somehow it rolled past Blake and into the net.  Out of nothing, Atlanta led 1-0 with 10 men.

Atlanta earned a free kick in the 34th minute, but Almirón's delivery was poor and Philadelphia cleared it with one touch.  Moments later, Muyumba stopped the next attack by bringing down Danley Jean Jacques with a hard tackle.

Philadelphia came again.  A dangerous ball came into the box.  Báez, on the six and facing the backline, tried to clear it first-time.  He mishit it towards his own goal catching Hoyos by surprise.  Hoyos reacted fast enough to prevent the own-goal.  Pierre then knocked Almirón down without a foul being called, and Báez answered by stripping Sullivan inside Atlanta's box.

In another play, Almirón was surrounded by four Philadelphia players.  He flicked the ball over one of them and brought it back down before Damiani clipped him from behind in what looked similar to Mihaj's red.  Chapman reached towards his pocket, then put the card away.  Philadelphia pushed Atlanta straight back again, with all 10 players defending inside their own third.

Philadelphia threw everything forward before halftime.  Lukic found Damiani inside the box.  Damiani turned, and his shot was blocked for a corner.  Wagner sent the corner into the box, and Pierre met it with a beautiful header from about 12 yards that thunders off the bar.  Picault cleared the rebound.

Atlanta won a free kick, but Jacob lazily hit it forward towards Almirón and Picault.  Philadelphia easily won the ball back.

The ball pinballed around Atlanta's box in stoppage time.  Muyumba reacted first, spinning and sweeping it away along the ground before Philadelphia could bring it under control and shoot.  Philadelphia came straight back.  Sullivan followed with a wonderful shot, driving the ball hard and low towards the back corner.  Hoyos somehow got down and knocked it away.  Philadelphia found another open ball at the far post. The header went back across goal and bounced off the back post.

Atlanta's defenders were working desperately.  Philadelphia should have been ahead by several goals.  Luck was going Atlanta's way.

## {{ site.data.language.match_day_2nd_half }}

Philadelphia resumed where it had left off.  In a training ground move, kickoff was sent back to the keeper.  Blake carried the ball forward to nearly the center circle before sending it long.  Philly announced its intent by pressing high and hard, and kept kept everyone forward from there.

Philadelphia won yet another corner. It already felt like only a matter of time before one went in. The ball reached Westfield on the back post.  He headed the ball off Báez.  He knew little about the deflection, but he had put himself in the right position for the block.

Atlanta had been pressed extremely deep, with all nine outfield players defending within 30 yards of goal.

Westfield and Báez became tangled under a high ball inside the box.  Báez backed into position before Westfield collided with him from behind.  Chapman allowed play to continue, which looked like the right call.  Jacob then flew across with a long tackle to clear the next attack, and Edwards broke up the wave that followed.

Atlanta had a chance for a forward run in the 53rd minute.  Miranchuk, near the 18, played the ball forward to Almirón.  Harriel knocked him over immediately and caught him with a knee to the left thigh.  Chapman showed yellow while Almirón remained on the ground with what looked like a dead leg.

Reilly and Muyumba took turns stepping out of Atlanta's shape to attack the ball. One would press hard and firm while the other held his position, then both retreated quickly after their press. It looked planned, giving Atlanta a way to challenge the ball without opening the center.

Philadelphia still pushed Atlanta deeper. By the 60th minute, every Atlanta player was packed between 15 and 25 yards from net.

Philadelphia added fresh legs in the 57th minute, bringing on Quinn Sullivan and Jesús Bueno for Indiana Vassilev and Jovan Lukic. Sullivan gave Philadelphia another runner around the box, while Bueno helped keep the ball moving against Atlanta's retreating midfield.

Hoyos had collected a chipped ball and then a high cross, giving Atlanta brief breaks from the pressure.  The next time he caught the ball, he held it too long and Chapman awarded Philadelphia a corner.  Hoyos argued the decision, Chapman appeared visibly angry and showed him a yellow.  Iloski sent the corner in from the left.  Wagner reached it but headed well over.  Atlanta had been very lucky.

Philly were throwing everything at goal, but Atlanta's defense were holding firm.  And for the next few minutes, Atlanta were beginning to push forward.

Atlanta produced its best passing sequence of the half.  Around six players touched the ball before Almirón drove it along the six towards Picault.  Philadelphia knocked it behind, and Almirón took the corner from the left.  Philadelphia cleared, but Atlanta kept the ball in its half.  Alonso found Miranchuk in a tight space.  Miranchuk turned away from his defender and played the ball to Muyumba, who had continued moving forward after combining with Alonso.

Muyumba drove into the open space and looked up from outside the box.  It was difficult to see what opening he spotted.  He curled a long right-footed shot around the defenders and beyond Blake, sending it inside the right post.  It was a fantastic goal. Atlanta led 2-0 with 10 men.  The entire team ran to celebrate with the bench and coaches.

A minute later, Damiani clipped Jacob from behind as he ran towards a ball inside the box. Jacob jumped up and charged at him, then went back down for treatment. Chapman showed Damiani yellow.

Philadelphia pushed forward with renewed vigor.  Hoyos collected a long ball over the top, and Picault found himself surrounded by three players when Atlanta tried to get out. Jean Jacques then launched a shot from outside the arc.  It went high and wide to the right, but not by much.

Philadelphia made two more changes in the 71st minute. Agustín Anello replaced Harriel, and striker Ezekiel Alladoh came on for Damiani. Philadelphia had taken off a defender and added another forward.

Two minutes later, Wagner sent the ball in from the left.  Edwards got a slight touch, but the deflection fell to Iloski.  He met it with a nifty outside-of-the-foot flick to beat Hoyos.  Atlanta's lead was down to 2-1.

Philadelphia kept sending high balls into Atlanta's box.  The clearances became more frantic, and Atlanta's defenders looked completely spent.  

Atlanta changed two players in the 78th minute.  Luke Brennan replaced Picault, Stian Gregersen replaced Jacob.  Gregersen nearly made an immediate impact at the other end. He met Almirón's free kick with a header that went just over, although the flag went up afterward. Moments later, he was back inside Atlanta's box, beating Iloski to the ball before the forward could reach it.

Sullivan met the next ball with a volley towards the far post. Hoyos threw out one hand and pushed it away. Philadelphia kept the ball alive, and it bounced around Atlanta's box until the defenders put it behind for another corner.  Sullivan found space again a few minutes later.  He was open about eight yards from goal and hit the ball first-time with his left foot.  It flew high into the side netting.  For a moment, it looked like Philadelphia had equalized.

Arif Kovac replaced Almirón in the 84th minute and made his Atlanta United debut.  Almirón had been beaten up throughout the match and looked exhausted as he came off.  Kovac was knocked to the ground on his first touch.  Two minutes later, the forward was back near the 20-yard line defending.  Hernández replaced Muyumba, but he and Edwards appeared confused about their positioning. A few minutes later, Philadelphia nearly scored after Atlanta failed to settle the ball, and Hernández desperately cleared it.

Eight minutes went up on the board.  

Brennan fouled Sullivan in a dangerous area in the first minute of stoppage time.  Báez then stood in front of the free kick, then fell theatrically as Philadelphia tried to take it.  Chapman showed him a yellow card.  Hoyos is able to clear the resulting kick.

Westfield floated a cross into the box.  Iloski met it with a header from about eight yards, but sent it wide of the left post.  Atlanta was lucky again.

Wagner and Hernández dueled for the ball on the next attack, and Atlanta won it and cleared.  Philadelphia sent a long ball back into the box in the 95th minute.  Hernández headed it out, but Philadelphia put it straight back in.  Bodies fell as they fought for the loose ball before Báez knocked it away.

Atlanta was less than three minutes from the final whistle when Philadelphia committed everyone forward again.  Iloski helped move the ball to Wagner, who sent a bouncing cross into the box.  Pierre had been left open in the center.  He moved towards the ball and powered a header into the bottom-left corner.

One week after Pierre made his first MLS start and recorded his first assist, the young Homegrown scored his first MLS goal and pulled Philadelphia level in the 96th minute.

Philadelphia smelled blood and pushed everyone forward.  Seventy-one seconds later, Wagner launched a very long ball into the box.  Alladoh stretched out his left leg and somehow redirected it towards the same bottom corner Pierre had just found. His first MLS goal was pure reflex.  Absolute scenes inside **Subaru Park**.

Philly finally sit deep, to protect their lead and close out the half for the 3-2 win.

## {{ site.data.language.match_day_ending }}

Philadelphia never stopped attacking.  It kept sending players forward, kept adding fresh attackers and kept forcing Atlanta to defend one more ball.  Even after falling behind 2-0, the Union fought to every last man.  They never stopped believing.

Atlanta's players had been visibly exhausted and making mental mistakes long before Martino made his changes.  Almirón had been beaten up throughout the night, and the defenders were struggling to clear the box.  Martino waited too long to bring on fresh legs.

When asked about his options for preserving the lead, Martino said he did not want to change tactics and acknowledged that several players were too tired to continue. He did not explain why he waited until the 78th minute to make his first substitutions.
