Atlanta United started the 2026 season hoping that Tata Martino's return would restore some of what the club had lost in recent years.  The club's first manager, who led the club to the 2018 MLS Cup championship, was back.  And a rebuilding roster offered hope that the Five Stripes could return to playoff contention.

Ten months later, Atlanta is facing another failed season.

Wednesday night's [2-1 loss to New York City FC](/2026/10/28/atlutd-vs-nycfc/) at Citi Field officially eliminated Atlanta United from playoff contention.  For the first time in club history, Atlanta will miss the MLS Cup Playoffs in consecutive seasons.

The loss followed a pattern that had developed early in the year.  Atlanta created opportunities but struggled to finish them, while individual mistakes at the other end continued to cost the team points.  Martino and his players recognized these problems throughout the season, but they didn't always agree on what was causing them.  Despite roster changes, tactical adjustments, and repeated signs of improvement, many of those same problems were still costing Atlanta matches in October.

## Martino's Changing Assessment of Atlanta

Martino's comments show how his assessment of Atlanta changed over the course of the season.  Early concerns about the team's approach gave way to problems with its attacking play as well as defending.  By October, he was struggling to explain why Atlanta continued losing despite the improvements he saw in the team's performances.

Players questioned whether Atlanta knew when to keep attacking and when to focus on defending a lead.

## Rebuilding a Team Mid-Season

Atlanta started the season with a rebuilt roster, but made another round of significant changes during the summer.  During the early months, Martino used different players and formations, averaging roughly two and a half changes to the starting lineup between matches.  Injuries and lack of depth limited his options in July and August.  Atlanta averaged fewer than two starting lineup changes per match during those months, with young players such as Cooper Sanchez and Will Reilly receiving significant playing time.

The arrival of Breel Embolo, Mauricio Amaro, and Giuliano Galoppo gave Martino more options, and all three quickly became regular starters.  Sanchez saw his playing time decrease, while other players lost their places in the lineup.  Fafà Picault started seven of eight matches during July and August, then didn't start any of Atlanta's last ten.  Cayman Togashi, who had already seen limited playing time, made no appearances during September or October.

By September, Martino believed he finally had the competitive squad he wanted, although Atlanta had already fallen well behind in the playoff race.  He continued making roughly four substitutions per match, but his decisions didn't always reflect the depth he now had available.  Against NYCFC on October 28, with Atlanta fighting to keep its playoff hopes alive, Martino used just two substitutes despite having several attacking and midfield options available.  His decision to leave three substitutions unused raised questions about how much confidence he had in the players outside his preferred lineup.

After Atlanta lost its first three matches, Martino was asked whether he would make changes to his lineup.  He argued that replacing players only made sense if their replacements could improve the team.  He also believed changes needed to account for the players' development and confidence.

{% include quote.html

    speaker="Tata Martino, Atlanta United Head Coach"

## Closing Thoughts

After Atlanta's October 28 loss to NYCFC, Matt Edwards spoke about the frustration of continuing to discuss progress after another defeat.  Martino had pointed to improvements in the team's performances, while Edwards recognized how difficult it was to keep talking about building towards something when the results weren't improving.

{% include quote.html
   mode=""
   quote="But I think it's tough to keep saying that we're building onto something because it's frustrating to hear for everyone around."
   speaker="Matt Edwards, Atlanta United Defender"
   source="October 28, 2026 Post-Game Press Conference"
   image="/images/quotes/Matt Edwards 2026.webp"
%}

Throughout the season, Martino and his players kept returning to the same problems.  In February, players talked about concentration.  By April, Martino was discussing poor finishing and defensive mistakes, while others raised concerns about mentality and movement in attack.  In May, players spoke about protecting leads and managing matches better.  By October, many of those problems remained.

Atlanta showed it could play better.  The victories against Portland and Montréal came when the team defended well and made the most of its opportunities.  Cooper Sanchez also gained valuable experience, and the summer signings improved the squad.  Yet Atlanta struggled to produce those performances from one match to the next.

Martino acknowledged some of his mistakes, but he struggled to find solutions to the problems that continued all year.  His tactics, lineup decisions, and substitutions deserve scrutiny, just as the players must answer for missed chances, defensive mistakes, and lapses in concentration.  The sporting department also bears responsibility for a roster that lacked depth for much of the year and required significant changes during the summer.

After Atlanta was eliminated from playoff contention, Martino addressed the disappointment felt by the club's supporters after the failed season.
