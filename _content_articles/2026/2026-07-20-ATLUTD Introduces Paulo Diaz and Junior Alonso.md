[Júnior Alonso](/2026/07/13/junior-alonso/) and [Paulo Díaz](/2026/07/14/paulo-diaz/) were formally introduced Monday as Atlanta’s newest center backs, two experienced South American internationals brought into a team still trying to pull itself out of one of the most difficult stretches in club history.

{% include quote.html
   mode=""
   quote="Today’s an important day for Atlanta United.  We welcome two proven international defenders in Júnior Alonso and Paulo Díaz. Their quality, leadership, winning mentality will set the standard at our club."
   speaker="Chris Henderson, Chief Soccer Officer and Sporting Director"
   source=""
   image="/images/quotes/Chris Henderson 2026.webp"
%}

Atlanta signed Alonso, 33, on a free transfer from Atlético Mineiro through the 2028-29 season. Díaz, 31, arrived a day later from River Plate on a contract through 2027-28 with options for the following two seasons. Between them, they have made more than 750 club appearances.

But Atlanta’s interest was not based only on experience. The club also expects Alonso and Díaz to bring leadership, defensive organization and a higher standard to the group.

---
## Starting With The Day To Day 

Both defenders arrive knowing exactly where Atlanta United stands. The Five Stripes returned from the World Cup break Friday in Nashville with a makeshift back line that held up better than expected, but still left with a 1-0 loss. Atlanta created enough opportunities to stay in the match, but once again failed to turn an improved performance into points.

Alonso said there are no shortcuts out of Atlanta’s current situation.

{% include quote.html
   mode=""
   quote="I think, in soccer, there are no secrets.  So it all comes down to just hard work."
   speaker="Júnior Alonso, Atlanta United Defender"
   source="Introductory Press Conference"
   image="/images/quotes/Júnior Alonso 2026.webp"
%}


For Alonso, that work starts in training.  It means demanding more of himself and his teammates, raising the standard each day and building habits that eventually carry into matches.

Díaz offered much the same assessment.

{% include quote.html
   mode=""
   quote="I think it’s about the day to day.  I think about the mentality, about having positive energy."
   speaker="Paulo Díaz, Atlanta United Defender"
   source="Introductory Press Conference"
   image="/images/quotes/Paulo Díaz 2026.webp"
%}

Both emphasized that improvement would depend on the standards set every day in training and carried into matches.  For Alonso, Atlanta’s current situation was part of the appeal.  He acknowledged that, from a purely sporting perspective, joining a successful team is often easier. Atlanta offered something different.

{% include quote.html
   mode=""
   quote="For me, this is a chance [to] show my worth and show how I can help the team."
   speaker="Júnior Alonso, Atlanta United Defender"
   source="Introductory Press Conference"
   image="/images/quotes/Júnior Alonso 2026.webp"
%}

He had already spoken with Paraguayan national team teammate Miguel Almirón about Atlanta, the club and the situation surrounding the team. Once negotiations began, Alonso started following Atlanta more closely and learning about the players who could soon become his teammates.

---
## Leadership Behind A Young Team

Four Academy products started Friday in Nashville, including 21-year-old Tomás Jacob at center back. Jacob produced one of Atlanta’s strongest performances of the night, with Tata Martino later calling him the best player on the field.

Alonso said one of the responsibilities he and Díaz can take on is helping younger teammates better understand game situations and the tactical concepts Atlanta is trying to execute.

{% include quote.html
   mode=""
   quote="The club has a lot of young players. We think that they’re very young, but they need leaders to be able to help them understand the moments of the game, to understand the concept."
   speaker="Júnior Alonso, Atlanta United Defender"
   source="Introductory Press Conference"
   image="/images/quotes/Júnior Alonso 2026.webp"
%}

Díaz pointed to his time at River Plate, where he played alongside former Atlanta United defender Leandro González Pírez. He said their partnership depended on constant communication, with both defenders organizing the back line and giving instructions to the players around them.

That is the type of presence Atlanta expects both newcomers to provide.

Henderson sees that ability to organize and influence teammates as one of the reasons both defenders were brought in.

{% include quote.html
   mode=""
   quote="They help in the build, they help bring leadership in the back, helping play out of the back.  Both players are players who make other players around them better."
   speaker="Chris Henderson, Chief Soccer Officer and Sporting Director"
   source=""
   image="/images/quotes/Chris Henderson 2026.webp"
%}

That expectation has been consistent since the signings were announced. Henderson described Alonso as a natural leader who could add stability to the back line, while Díaz was brought in to add toughness, competition and experience.

---
## What Comes Next

Both defenders also fit Martino’s preference for an aggressive, high defensive line that requires center backs to be comfortable defending in space.

{% include quote.html
   mode=""
   quote="We know that the style that we want to play is to be on the front foot, and we know that comes with risks as defenders."
   speaker="Júnior Alonso, Atlanta United Defender"
   source="Introductory Press Conference"
   image="/images/quotes/Júnior Alonso 2026.webp"
%}

Their arrival also increases competition at center back. Henderson said Alonso and Díaz are not guaranteed places, pointing to Stian Gregersen, Enea Mihaj and Tomás Jacob as part of a deeper group competing for minutes.

{% include quote.html
   mode="important"
   quote="My history in the league with good teams is you have guys who are left every week sitting on the bench who could be starting, should be starting, but they can’t get in the team. So I think that’s how we want to change things going forward."
   speaker="Chris Henderson, Chief Soccer Officer and Sporting Director"
   source=""
   image="/images/quotes/Chris Henderson 2026.webp"
%}

Alonso has received his visa and is eligible for selection this week. Atlanta is still completing the process for Díaz.

The two defenders are only the beginning of Atlanta’s summer work. Henderson said the club is in discussions over multiple positions farther up the field, with some incoming moves tied to outgoing players.

The first additions addressed the back line. The next moves will come higher up the pitch.
